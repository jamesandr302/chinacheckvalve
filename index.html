Step 1: Confirm the company is real and makes valves

The public registry proves the legal entity exists. It does not prove capability, which is why the later steps matter.

  1. 1. Get the business license and its 18-character Unified Social Credit Code. Search that code on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (GSXT, gsxt.gov.cn), the official registry run by China's market regulator. The interface is Chinese-only, and deeper records may need a login, so a local colleague or a verification service often does this step.
  2. 2. Read the record, not just the license. The status should be active. Cancelled or revoked entries mean the company no longer operates. The legal name must match your contract character for character.
  3. 3. Read the business scope. It should include valve manufacturing, and import and export rights if they will ship directly. A scope of consulting or trading, with no manufacturing, tells you the seller is a middleman.
  4. 4. Match four names. The license, the contract party, the bank account holder and the invoice issuer should be the same entity. If the factory asks you to pay a different company, get the relationship and the warranty-liable entity in writing before sending money.
  5. 5. Read the ISO 9001 scope line. A manufacturer's certificate says design, manufacture or production. A trader's says sales or trading. Step 2 shows how to confirm the certificate is genuine.
  6. 6. Ask for a live video walk-through. The contact should walk from the casting or machining area to assembly and the test bench, and point the camera at a gauge and a valve under test. Recorded tours show nothing about today.

Check the address on the license against the certificates and the shipping documents. A factory in an industrial zone and a sales office in an apartment block are different things.

How to check an API license

  1. 1. Ask for the license number and the PDF.
  2. 2. Search the API Composite List by company name or number.
  3. 3. Compare the licensed site address, the product scope and the expiry date with the certificate.
  4. 4. If there is no match, ask the factory to explain before going further. API also publishes a fraud-alert list of parties that misuse its marks.

Read the scope line for check valves. One PED Module H certificate we reviewed covers butterfly, gate, globe, check and ball valves. Its limit for check valves is PN110 and DN600 (Class 600, 24 in). Ball valves on the same certificate reach PN150. A buyer who read only the company name would assume the same ceiling for every type.

Three quick tests apply to every certificate. The holder must be the seller or the named manufacturer. The address must match the factory. The expiry date must be in the future.

Step 3: Match the factory to your valve type and standard

A factory that lists gate, globe, ball, butterfly and check valves is normal. What matters is evidence for the one you need. Ask for a general arrangement drawing of your valve type, two reference orders in the same size and pressure class, and a test record for that type. A factory that casts swing check bodies owns the patterns for them, so ask to see a casting. The same logic applies to a China Butterfly valves factory: judge it on proof for the exact type, not on the catalog.

Know which standard your datasheet means. Suppliers often answer with a different standard from the one asked for.

Ask type-specific questions. Each design fails in a different place.

The factory cannot fix a wrong selection. Check valve slam occurs when the disc closes after reverse flow has built up. The resulting surge can fatigue welds and crack bodies over time. Give the factory your line length, pump data and closing requirement, and ask which type it recommends and why.

Step 4: Audit the casting source, machining and test bench

Ask who casts the bodies. Many valve factories buy castings from outside foundries. That is acceptable if it is controlled. Ask for the foundry's name and address, confirm the factory's quality system approves it, and check that castings carry the foundry mark and a heat or cast number. End users' approved-manufacturer lists are specific to the factory, the foundry and the forge shop, so a quiet change of foundry can break an approval. Inspectors confirm castings come from the approved foundry for this reason.

AreaWhat to seeRed flag

Know what the test should look like. API 598 covers shop inspection and testing for check valves. The shell test pressurizes the body with water at 1.5 times rated pressure and allows no visible leakage. The closure test applies pressure in the direction that closes the disc, at 1.1 times rated pressure. Most check valves have no stem, so there is no backseat test.

Seat tightness depends on the seat type. Resilient-seated check valves must show no visible leakage. Metal-seated check valves are allowed a small leakage that scales with size. A passed test on a metal-seated swing check does not mean it is drip-tight. ISO 5208 grades leakage from Rate A (none) to Rate G, and your purchase order must state the rate you require.

Ask the factory to run a closure test while you watch. Then ask what happens to a valve that fails. A factory that scraps or reworks it and records the failure is controlling quality. One that says nothing ever fails is not.

Choose how to audit. Visit in person, run a live virtual audit, or hire a third-party agency such as SGS, Bureau Veritas, TÜV or Intertek. For a first order, audit before the deposit, not after.

Step 5: Match documents to the physical valve

Paper only counts if it ties to the valve you receive. Ask for these records and check the details listed.

Do not mix up two kinds of certificate. EN 10204 describes how material test results are documented. It is not a valve performance test. A buyer who asks for "3.2" expecting a witnessed pressure test will get the wrong document. State both requirements separately: the material certificate type, and the test standard with its leakage rate.

Specify 3.2 before the material is bought. An independent witness must see the tests. A factory holding 3.1 material from its suppliers cannot upgrade it after the fact.

Test the paper against a valve. Pick a body from the lot, read the heat number on the casting, and find it on the certificate. Check that documents carry your purchase order number and are dated before shipment. A certificate whose heat number appears on no valve is only paper.

Step 6: Check the export record, run a pilot order and witness the inspection

Check export experience. Ask for two customer references, preferably an EPC contractor or distributor, who bought the same valve type in a similar class. Ask which markets the factory ships to and which certificate route each market needs: PED and CE for the EU, an API license for many pipeline specifications. Sample export paperwork with customer names redacted shows the factory handles shipping documents. It shows paperwork skill, not valve quality, so treat it as supporting evidence only.

Order a pilot batch from production. A hand-picked sample proves what the factory can do on its best day. Buy production units in the exact size, class and material you plan to order.

Set hold points before shipment. For check valves, witness or have an inspector witness:

  1. 1. Shell test at the specified pressure.
  2. 2. Closure test, plus the gas closure test if your specification calls for it.
  3. 3. Free movement of the disc and hinge, and the spring on spring-assisted types.
  4. 4. Markings: size, rating, body material, heat number, flow arrow and manufacturer.
  5. 5. Dimensions, including face-to-face and flange drilling.
  6. 6. PMI on a sample of the lot.

Give the inspector your datasheet, the governing standard, the required leakage rate and the acceptance criteria. Third-party agencies such as SGS, Bureau Veritas, TÜV and Intertek work from what you supply. Review the full document package before you release the shipment.

Tie the money to the result. Keep the deposit modest and pay the balance against a passed inspection report and the documents. Use a letter of credit or a similar secured method where you can.

Repeat the checks on repeat orders. Approvals are not permanent. Re-check certificate expiry dates and ask whether the foundry has changed before each new order.

Red flags that should end the process

  • • The certificate holder or licensed site differs from the seller.
  • • Check valves are missing from the certificate scope, or your size or class exceeds its limit.
  • • An API claim that does not appear in the API Composite List.
  • • The factory cannot name its foundry, or will not show foundry marks on castings.
  • • A "pressure tested" statement with no closure test record.
  • • Heat numbers on the certificate that match no valve in the lot.
  • • A refusal of a live video call, an audit or third-party inspection.
  • • The supplier answers "API 594" when your datasheet says "API 6D", or the reverse, and cannot explain the difference.
  • • A price far below other quotes, with no explanation of what changed: seat type, forged or cast body, rating, or material grade.
  • • Pressure for a large deposit, or documents withheld until payment.

Decision rule and printable checklist

Steps 1 and 2 are knockouts. One "no" ends the process. For steps 3 to 6 on critical service, such as pipelines, high pressure or hazardous fluids, treat every item as required. For general water or HVAC duty you can accept a conditional pass where a gap has a clear fix, such as a late document, as long as it is closed before shipment.

FAQ

How do I verify that a Chinese check valve supplier is legitimate?

Match the business license and credit code to the official GSXT registry record, confirm each certificate with its issuer, and check that check valves appear in the certificate scope. Then watch a live walk-through and test a pilot batch before volume orders.

Which certifications should a check valve factory hold?

It depends on duty and market. ISO 9001 is the baseline. Pipeline and oil and gas work often calls for an API license such as API 6D. EU sales need PED and CE documentation. Potable water needs NSF/ANSI 61 materials. In every case, the scope must list check valves.

Should I specify API 594 or API 6D for a check valve?

Use the one on your datasheet. API 594 covers wafer, wafer-lug and double-flanged check valves plus bolted-cover swing check valves. API 6D covers pipeline and piping valves, including check valves.

Is a check valve the same as a non-return valve?

Yes. The function is identical. "Non-return valve" is the more common term in British and European specifications.

Are Chinese check valves as good as those from other countries?

Origin does not decide it. Documented standards compliance, material traceability and witnessed test records do. A factory that provides all three can meet the same specification as any other. One that cannot should not get the order.

How long does verification take?

The document checks in steps 1 and 2 are quick, often a day or two with a local contact. The audit and pilot order depend on the factory's production lead time.
